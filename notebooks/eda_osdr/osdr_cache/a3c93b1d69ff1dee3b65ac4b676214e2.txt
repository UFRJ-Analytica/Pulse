Life detection missions to these sensitive locations must adhere to NASA\u2019s planetary protection requirements. These requirements include probabilistic analyses for contamination on the target body as well as limits on biological contamination (i.e., bioburden) on and in the spacecraft. Historically, NASA has relied heavily upon heat microbial reduction (HMR) to reduce bioburden embedded within nonmetallic materials used in spacecraft assemblies. However, given the long transit time expected for spacecraft aiming to reach Europa or Enceladus, there is additional risk that the harshness of the space environment coupled with HMR may adversely impact such materials. When a bioburden reduction method like HMR cannot be employed, NASA relies upon bioburden assumptions known as \u201cspecification values\u201d to calculate bioburden. These values are known to be conservative, potentially inflating the true bioburden associated with materials. Therefore, there is an increased need for alternative bioburden evaluation methods for nonmetallic materials when HMR cannot be used. In this study, we compared the quantification approaches of culture-based methods with metagenomic analysis to evaluate the bioburden associated with 17 nonmetallic materials. We extracted DNA directly from nonmetallic materials and performed qPCR coupled with metagenomic sequencing. Isolated microbes from the culture-based assay were identified using a combination of 16S rRNA Sanger sequencing and MALDI-TOF MS. The results from this work demonstrate that while low biomass represents a challenge, metagenomics may be a feasible approach for supplementing culture-based methods for assessing bioburden.
